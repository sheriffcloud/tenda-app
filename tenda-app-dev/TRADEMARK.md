# Tenda trademark policy

The code in this repository is licensed in two parts — `contracts/` under
Apache-2.0, everything else under BUSL-1.1 (see [LICENSING.md](LICENSING.md)).
The Tenda **name and marks are not licensed by either**. Apache-2.0 §6 grants
no trademark rights, BUSL-1.1 says the same, and this document states plainly
what that means so nobody has to infer it.

## What is not licensed

- The name **Tenda**, alone or as part of another name.
- The Tenda logo, wordmark and app icon, in any colour or variation.
- The domain `tenda.so` and any confusingly similar domain.
- The Tenda app listings and their store presence.

## What you may do

- Use, modify, run and redistribute the **contracts**, including commercially,
  under Apache-2.0.
- Use, modify and redistribute the **apps** under BUSL-1.1, and run your own
  instance — for your own organisation, for evaluation or research, or to
  settle transactions you are a party to — **under your own name**.
- Say truthfully that your software is *"built on Tenda"* or *"a fork of
  Tenda"*. Nominative reference to describe origin is expressly permitted by
  Apache-2.0 §6, and this policy permits it for the BUSL-licensed parts too.
- Use the name in commentary, documentation, research and comparison.

## What you may not do

- Name, brand or market your fork as Tenda, or as anything close enough to be
  mistaken for it.
- Use the Tenda logo or wordmark in your fork's UI, store listing or site.
- Imply that your deployment is operated, endorsed or supported by Tenda.

Note that the apps carry a **licence** limit as well as this trademark one:
BUSL-1.1 does not permit offering them to third parties as a commercial escrow,
payments or dispute-resolution service, whatever you call it. That restriction
lapses two years after each release, when that version becomes Apache-2.0. The
contracts carry no such limit at any point.

If you run a fork, remove the Tenda name and marks from the interface and
release it under your own brand. The code is yours to use on the terms above;
the identity is how users tell your deployment apart from ours, and that
distinction protects them as much as it protects us.

Questions, or want permission for something not covered here? Ask us.
